# Public Site Plan

This file defines what `heyvera.org` should communicate and how it
should feel.

## Core Job

The public site should make a new visitor understand:
- AI should belong to the person using it
- HeyVera gives that person a named agent
- Vera improves that agent through shared intelligence
- Soma gives continuity, authority, and proof underneath
- this is the public mode of a larger living surface

## Big Design Idea

Do not build this like a generic long-form brochure.

Build it like:
- the public front door to a larger product world
- a calm, premium, code-native surface
- a place where the future app feels implied by the design language

The page should feel like a real destination, not a placeholder before
"the actual app."

## Tone

The site should feel:
- premium
- calm
- technical
- modern
- trustworthy
- a little cinematic, but restrained

It should not feel:
- like a template AI startup page
- like a crypto casino
- like a dashboard promo site
- like an overhyped venture landing page

## Current Scope Rule

For the first public release:
- keep it as one excellent page
- do not add docs pages, blog pages, or pricing
- do not add auth flows or signed-in app UI
- do not add fake product screens beyond one disciplined surface preview

## Copy Hierarchy

### Navbar

Brand:
- `HeyVera`

Links:
- `Why`
- `Surface`
- `How It Works`
- `Founding`

CTA:
- `Get Started`

### Hero

Headline:
`Your AI. Your name. Your sovereignty.`

Support should explain:
- you own a named agent
- it runs on your hardware
- Vera improves it through shared intelligence
- Soma gives continuity and proof underneath

Hero proof / preview element:
- not just decorative fake code
- not a fake full app mock
- one believable surface preview that combines:
  - region labels or tabs
  - a focused `Work` state
  - terminal or conversation proof
  - small local/proof/status markers

Interpretation:
- the hero should feel like a real surface that could later deepen into
  the signed-in product

Hero guardrails:
- show one primary active region, not six open panels
- do not use charts, admin tables, wallet dashboards, or dense sidebars
- do not make it look like a crypto terminal
- keep the preview emotionally legible in under 3 seconds

### Problem

Heading idea:
`Most AI still is not really yours.`

This section should explain:
- rented assistants forget context
- platforms trap the learning
- updates break reliability
- the user does not really own the relationship

### Surface Map

Heading idea:
`One surface. Distinct regions.`

This section is the bridge to the future product.

It should preview six regions:
- Work
- Network
- Discover
- Proof
- Identity
- Markets

Each preview should be short and structural, not feature soup.

This is where Pulse-like social energy and ClawNet-like system breadth
get reinterpreted into one clean worldview.

Surface map guardrails:
- one sentence or phrase per region, not mini feature lists
- no fake stats, no fake activity feeds, no fake product screenshots
- `Markets` should read as one contained region, not the headline
- the section should explain the world shape, not simulate the whole app

### Three Pillars

Use these anchors:
- Own the agent
- Learn through Vera
- Prove through Soma

### How It Works

Three steps:
- Name your agent
- Work locally
- Contribute verified learning

### Founding Network

Purpose:
- frame the early social layer correctly
- make it clear that real work shapes the network
- feel like a serious founding circle, not hype community bait

### Get Started

One action only:
- waitlist placeholder
- or GitHub action if that is the honest Day 0 move

Do not turn the CTA into a fake funnel.

## Visual System

### Palette

- page background near `#09090b`
- raised surface near `#111114`
- strong text near `#f5f7fa`
- muted text near `#a7b0bb`
- border near `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`
- accent near `#7ce0c3`

Accent rule:
- use the accent for focus, proof chips, active states, and one primary
  CTA
- do not wash the whole page in accent color

### Typography

- headlines: `Space Grotesk` or another strong modern sans
- body: `Inter`
- mono: `JetBrains Mono`

Typography character:
- headlines should feel declarative and modern
- body should feel calm and readable
- mono should signal proof and runtime reality

### Layout

- one-column story rhythm by default
- generous vertical breathing room
- central content column with occasional wider surface moments
- rounded panels and clean borders
- no cramped card walls

### Atmosphere

- very subtle grid or radial texture
- restrained glow
- crisp panel edges
- believable terminal treatment
- no floating blobs or fake AI art

### Motion

- section reveals should be subtle
- hover states should feel precise
- no looping spectacle

### Art Direction Timing

For the first build:
- prioritize structure, hierarchy, spacing, and atmosphere
- do not block implementation on custom illustration or bespoke art

Later polish can add:
- richer visual motifs
- illustration
- more advanced motion
- stronger branded proof graphics

## Component Intent

`Navbar`
- calm, confident, not busy

`Hero`
- strongest section on the page
- should feel like the page's design thesis

`Problem`
- resets the visitor's mental model fast

`SurfaceMap`
- the most important new addition
- proves this is a product world, not a one-feature app

`Pillars`
- memorable, conceptual anchors

`HowItWorks`
- practical and grounded

`FoundingNetwork`
- where the future social layer first appears

`GetStarted`
- one clear next step

## Mobile Truth

On phone, the first screen should show:
- brand
- main claim
- CTA
- a glimpse of the surface preview

Rules:
- everything stacks in one column
- the preview remains readable
- tabs or region labels wrap cleanly or scroll elegantly
- no dense desktop card walls collapsed onto phone

## Reuse / Avoid

Reuse from old ClawNet:
- dark surface craft
- terminal treatment
- spacing discipline

Reuse from Pulse carefully:
- hints of network life
- discovery energy

Avoid:
- purple-heavy styling
- dashboard patterns
- ceremony or roster admin visuals
- marketplace framing as the whole identity
- crypto-central storytelling

## Design Sanity Checks

Before calling the page done, ask:
- does the hero explain the product in under 10 seconds?
- does the page feel like a public mode of a larger world?
- does the surface-map section make the future shape legible?
- does the page avoid fake credibility tricks?
- does it still make sense to a smart non-technical visitor?
- does mobile feel equally intentional?

## Public-Site Quality Bar

The page is good enough when:
- the first impression is memorable
- the story order feels inevitable
- the hero preview looks believable
- the surface map feels useful, not decorative
- the page does not feel generic
- the page does not feel crypto-first
- the mobile layout feels designed
